# Brain update 6 from AnIdeaLab: bringing a big pile of files in, an agent per busy project in the weekly tidy-up, task notes in place of session hand-offs, the upload point as a plain right-click, and copied skills that stay up to date

Sent 11 Nov 2026. This update gives your brain a proper way to take in a lot of the owner's files at once, and the same idea for a busy week's tidy-up: split the work by subject, give each subject to an agent with a clear head, and have the coordinator join them up. It also gives each job one task note that's kept up to date, in place of a new hand-off note each time a chat gets long. The upload point becomes a plain right-click, and a skill your brain has brought up to date is no longer put back to an older copy. There is one command for the owner to paste, to clear an old desktop shortcut.

## Summary for the owner
- **Bringing a lot of files in:** until now your brain sorted dropped files one agent at a time, which suits a few files but not a whole folder: the agent's memory fills up, detail gets lost, and nobody can say afterwards which files were read. Now there's a method for a big pile. Your agent first takes a look at what's there (how many files, what kinds, which are copies), which costs almost none of your plan, then shows you a plan. For a big pile it gives each subject (a business, the house, tax) to its own agent, two at a time, and keeps a list of every file and what it became, so nothing is quietly missed. Your files stay where they are.
- **A free tool for reading documents:** to turn PDFs, Word, Excel and PowerPoint files into text, your agent installs Microsoft's free MarkItDown on your computer, the first time you bring a pile in. Your agent tells you when it does.
- **A busy week's tidy-up:** when one of your projects has moved a lot in a week, the weekly tidy-up now gives that project its own agent, so the rest of your brain still gets a careful read. A quiet week's tidy-up is the same as before, with no extra agents. It tells you first, because agents use more of your plan.
- **One note per job that's always up to date:** when a chat got long or a stage ended, your agent used to write a new hand-off note each time, and a busy job left a trail of them to read through. Now each job has one task note that your agent keeps up to date as it works: where the job stands, what's next, and what's waiting on you. When a chat gets long, the line you paste into a new chat is that note's path. Nothing changes in what you do.
- **Giving your brain files is now just a right-click:** select files or folders, right-click, Send to, My brain, and tell Claude what to do with them. The Upload point window and its desktop shortcut are gone; they did the same job with more to go wrong. Your files still stay where they are.
- **Skills your brain has improved stay improved:** your weekly tidy-up brings copied skills up to date from where they came from. An update used to put the kit's older copies back. Now it leaves them alone.
- **A fix for brains used on a Windows PC:** the link check wrongly said nearly every note had nothing linking to it when it ran on Windows. Fixed.

One thing to paste, once your PC's copy has synced after the update: a command your agent gives you that clears the old Upload point shortcut from your desktop. Nothing else runs on your PC.

## What changed, and why
1. **The file-intake skill.** New: `skills/file-intake/SKILL.md` and `skills/file-intake/intake.py`, with its line in `skills/lists/the-brain.md`. The skill has the steps, a table for picking the method by how many documents there are, how much text and what kinds, the brief for a subject agent and for the links agent, and the file list. The script surveys a pile (`survey`), turns its documents into text with MarkItDown where it's installed (`convert`), puts the files into subjects (`split`), and checks that every file has an outcome and every note names the file it came from (`check`). Why: one agent reading a whole pile gets things wrong and can't say what it missed. AnIdeaLab tried it on a made-up pile of 35 files in three subjects, with two subject agents and a links agent: every file was accounted for, and what those agents found unclear is in the skill.
2. **The first session asks about files.** `skills/getting-started/SKILL.md`: its questions now ask whether the owner has files to bring in, and a new part 7, "Their files, if they have some", says a few go through the upload point and a lot through file-intake, as a job of its own after setup. "Finish" is now part 8.
3. **The upload point is Send to, My brain, and nothing else.** `skills/upload-point/upload-point.ps1` is now the short script Send to runs: it adds each sent path to a plain text list on the PC, `C:\AI-Brain-Link\_upload\waiting.txt` (the time, a tab, the path), and shows a small box. Paths only, except anything on a removable drive, which is copied to `_upload\<date>\` first. `setup-upload-point.ps1` makes only the Send to entry and removes the old desktop shortcut. `look.ps1` reads the list and, with `-Done`, takes dealt-with paths off it. `skills/upload-point/SKILL.md` is rewritten: a session on the PC reads the list directly, a cloud session reads it with one job on the PC, and a drop of more than about 30 documents, or a folder mixing several projects, goes to file-intake. `knowledge/systems/upload-point.md` is no longer the list, only a short note of where it is. `skills/brain-spec/reminders.py` says what's waiting only in a session on the owner's PC; `skills/file-intake/intake.py`'s `--upload-list` reads the new list; `.github/workflows/library.yml` no longer skips a push of the old note; and the mentions in `README.md`, `knowledge/systems/my-pc.md`, `knowledge/systems/routines.md`, the starting rules note, `skills/getting-started/SKILL.md`, `skills/file-intake/SKILL.md` and `skills/lists/the-brain.md` say Send to. Why: the window kept its list on GitHub through a sign-in, re-read it every 30 seconds and had to handle clashes, all to do what a right-click and a text file do. A cloud session is no longer told at its start that something is waiting, so the owner says they've sent something.
4. **The weekly tidy-up gives a busy project its own agent.** `skills/brain-audit/SKILL.md`: a new section before "1. Clean up", "A busy project gets its own agent", and its description. A quiet week is unchanged. A project with more than about ten notes, tasks and hand-offs moved gets an agent that does the tidy-up's steps for that project alone and reports back; with two or more, a links agent reads the reports and says what crosses between projects, and the coordinator writes those. Why: a busy project's detail fills the one agent's memory, and the rest of the brain gets a tired read.
5. **The link check on Windows.** `skills/brain-spec/check_links.py`: a linked note's path is now compared with `/` on Windows too. Before, every note looked as if nothing linked to it.
6. **Task notes in place of session hand-offs.** `knowledge/tasks/README.md` has a new part, "A task note is the job's live status": the note's shape (a status that's rewritten in place: where it stands, next, waiting on, where everything is; and a record that only grows: the ask, decisions, watch-outs, progress), when to update it (when the job starts, when a stage or working piece is finished, when the owner decides something, when something starts waiting, before stopping), and how two chats share one with no lock (one holder writes the status, any other only adds a line or takes a piece with a note of its own, each update pulled, committed alone and pushed at once, and a clash keeps both sides' lines). `skills/handoff/SKILL.md` is rewritten round it: no new session hand-offs, the task note brought up to date at the end of a stage and at the nudge, its path given to paste, and how to start from one; briefs and reports are unchanged. `skills/handoff/nudge.json`'s three messages for the chat say the same (its numbers are unchanged). `knowledge/handoffs/README.md`, `skills/brain-spec/SKILL.md`, `skills/guide-mode/SKILL.md`, `skills/getting-started/SKILL.md`, `skills/brain-audit/SKILL.md`, `skills/file-intake/SKILL.md`, `skills/lists/the-brain.md` and the starting rules note point to it. Why: a new hand-off at each stage and each nudge copied the last one's open list, so a busy job left a trail to read through and nobody could say at a glance where it stood; one note that's rewritten stays true. The handoff skill also said a sub-agent's nudge was at 450,000 tokens in two places; it's 350,000, as `nudge.json` has it.
7. **The PC scripts download through GitHub's command line first.** `skills/my-pc/setup-runner.ps1` gets the runner with `gh release download`, falling back to the plain link, and `skills/my-pc/check-setup.ps1`'s fix for a missing Obsidian Git plugin is a `gh release download` command. Why: GitHub's plain release-download links can fail for hours while `gh`'s route works. Nothing runs now: the scripts only run when the owner sets the runner up again or runs the setup check.
8. **A copied skill never goes back to an older copy.** `skills/brain-spec/brain_update.py`: `compare` lists a copied skill that this brain has brought to its own commit of its source apart, as expected, and `compare --take` refuses its files. `apply` no longer merges a copied skill line by line: it replaces the changed files whole, keeping the skill's "In this brain" section and `LESSONS.md`, and leaves alone any source the brain has moved on, with its line in `skills/lists/sources.md`. `skills/brain-spec/SKILL.md`, "Brain updates", says the same. Why: a brain's tidy-up brought three copied skills up to date, and the next update's `compare` had the agent put the kit's older copies back; another brain's merge doubled a block inside a copied script. This update is applied by the `brain_update.py` the brain already has, so the new `apply` first runs at the update after this one; the new `compare` runs in this one. This update changes no copied skill.
9. **Small fixes from owners' reports.** `apply` now merges `knowledge/systems/brain-updates/README.md` (it skipped it without saying) and leaves the part between a shelf's index markers to `library.py build`, so indexes stop clashing (both from the update after this one). `brain_update.py check` and the daily notice say the summary is read to the owner first, even when they ask for the update by name. `skills/brain-spec/library.py audit --since` works in a shallow clone, as a cloud session has: it counts what the history holds and says what it couldn't see (`test_library.py` has a test for it). `skills/brain-spec/check_links.py` skips link syntax written as an example inside backticks. `skills/brain-audit/SKILL.md` has two lines brought into step.
10. **The skill count.** `README.md`, `knowledge/areas/the-brain/README.md`, `skills/find-skills/SKILL.md` and `skills/getting-started/SKILL.md` say 65 skills to start, not 64.

## What to do beyond the files
**Read the owner the summary above before anything else, and apply only on their go,** even if they asked for the update by name.

**Before applying, look at the old upload list.** Pull, and read "Waiting" in `knowledge/systems/upload-point.md`. If anything is there, sort it first, or note the paths and put each on the new list afterwards with one job on the PC: `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File "C:\AI-Brain-Link\<repo name>\skills\upload-point\upload-point.ps1" -Quiet "<path>"`. Ask the owner to close the Upload point window if it's open: an open one is the old code, and it would write its list back to GitHub.

1. **Clashes.** Save `apply`'s output and read both sides of each clash. The likely ones: `knowledge/areas/the-brain/README.md`, which holds this brain's own history (keep the brain's own lines, and don't change its count of skills if the brain has added its own); `README.md`, the front door, where the owner may have their own lines (keep them); and `skills/lists/the-brain.md`, if the brain added lines of its own (keep them, and add the file-intake line). In `skills/brain-audit/SKILL.md`, `skills/upload-point/SKILL.md` and `skills/getting-started/SKILL.md`, put back any lesson the tidy-up folded in.
2. **Ask the owner once, in a line, whether they have files they'd like the brain to know.** If yes, follow `skills/getting-started/SKILL.md` part 7: it's a job of its own, not part of this update. If no, nothing more.
3. **Don't install MarkItDown now.** The file-intake skill installs it the first time a pile is brought in, and tells the owner then.
4. **Don't start any agents now.** The next weekly tidy-up decides, from what moved that week, whether a project gets one.
5. **The upload point on the PC.** Send to, My brain keeps working with no reinstall once Obsidian Git has synced the PC's copy, since the script kept its name. Then give the owner this one command to paste into PowerShell, which clears the old desktop shortcut and makes the Send to entry again: `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File "C:\AI-Brain-Link\<repo name>\skills\upload-point\setup-upload-point.ps1"` (with their brain's folder name). If `knowledge/systems/upload-point.md` clashes, take the kit's: it's no longer the list. In `knowledge/systems/my-pc.md` and `knowledge/systems/routines.md`, keep the owner's own lines and take the kit's wording for the `_upload\` line and the "Upload point check" row. A brain whose copy isn't in `C:\AI-Brain-Link` passes `-List` to `look.ps1`, as the skill says. A brain set up on a Mac never had Send to: its files change and nothing else does.
6. **Copied skills.** Whatever the new `compare` lists apart as brought up to date in this brain needs nothing done: don't take the kit's copies back, and don't put a source's commit in `skills/lists/sources.md` back to the kit's. If an earlier update put older copies back, the next tidy-up's `skill_updates.py check` and `update` bring them forward again, and later updates leave them alone. For a copied skill with no source commit that `compare` lists apart, leaving it is a fine answer.
7. **Two things the old `apply` still does this once.** It skips `knowledge/systems/brain-updates/README.md`: put the kit's `about:` line and opening paragraph in by hand, keeping this brain's "On kit update" and "Skipped" lines and its index. And shelf indexes may clash: take either side and run `python3 skills/brain-spec/library.py build`.
8. **Task notes and hand-offs already in the brain.** Don't reshape the brain's task notes now: each takes the new shape the next time the chat holding it updates it. Leave session hand-offs already on the shelf as they are; an open one is still good to start from, and whoever picks it up moves where it stands into the job's task note, as the handoff skill says. If the owner changed the nudge's numbers in `skills/handoff/nudge.json`, keep their numbers and take the kit's three new messages (`message`, `urgent_message`, `prompt_message`). If `knowledge/tasks/README.md` or `knowledge/handoffs/README.md` clashes, the clash is in the index at the end: take the kit's page and run `python3 skills/brain-spec/library.py build`.
9. **Run `python3 skills/brain-spec/brain_update.py compare`,** and fix what it finds.

11. **Check the report went:** the "Send brain update reports" run for your push should say "Sent 0006-report.md" in its log. If it says anything else, run that workflow by hand.

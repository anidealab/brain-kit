## Summary for the owner
- **A big update: apply it in a new session, running Opus 5.5 on high effort.** If this chat isn't a new one on Opus 5.5 on high, start one (pick the model and effort in the chat's settings) and say "apply the brain update" there.
- **Every chat knows who you are, where you live and your time of day.** The brain's core now has a small table with your name, your town and your time zone, so a new chat doesn't have to ask again.
- **Your agents get on with things.** Inside the brain's few gates (spending, keys, messages to the outside, your voice), they decide and act without asking first. There's no rule about open-source licences any more.
- **Rules your agents write get checked with you.** Once a month the deep tidy-up lists any rule an agent wrote that limits what agents can do, with a line on what keeping it costs, and asks you: keep it or cut it.
- **The monthly check costs less as your brain grows.** It reads only that month's new decisions in full, against short summaries of the rest, instead of every decision every month.
- **The weekly tidy-up, when you say yes to it, catches what got saved in the wrong place:** your answers or lessons left in a finished task, old plans still reading as current, task notes that grew too long, code or videos in the brain. Your agents also now save things where the next chat will look, with examples of what goes wrong otherwise.
- **When a job needs your computer and it isn't set up yet,** the brain offers to set it up in a few easy steps, instead of working around it.
- **Files come back as downloads in the chat,** with where they are on your computer, instead of GitHub links. Nothing is kept only in one chat, so the next chat can always find it.
- **A computer you've set up is always found,** and the check of which computers are on also says each one's time zone, so the brain notices if you're away. With no computer set up, it says so straight away instead of after a minute's check.
- **The PC runner no longer needs your Windows password.** New setups start it hidden whenever you sign in to Windows. Yours keeps working as it is; to switch, your agent gives you one command to paste once (it asks for admin rights once, to take the old setup off). The trade-off: it runs while you're signed in (a locked screen is fine), not after a restart until you sign in.
- **Hand-off nudges, later and closer together, and in every chat:** a chat's first comes at 300,000 tokens, then one every 25,000, urgent from 400,000; an agent the chat hands work to gets the same, by its own conversation. - **Guide mode has a level, 1 to 4, for how much your agents explain.** It no longer assumes you're new to AI. Yours starts at 2 (pretty techy: fine copying and pasting commands), unless you've already told your brain how much you like explained; 1 explains every technical term, 4 talks shop. Change it any time ("put me on 3", "explain more"); the monthly deep tidy-up reminds you that you can, and nothing else does. Planning and check-ins stay the same at every level, since they're about your plan's usage. Turning guide mode down or off now just moves your level up; there's no separate tuned-down version, and the brain's core simply says it's on and sends agents to the guide-mode skill.
- **The brain graph opens in a second or two,** however big your brain gets. Before, it worked everything out again each time it opened, which took about a minute in a brain a few months old. Now it reads your history once and keeps what it read, checking itself as it goes. If it ever looks wrong, press Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on a Mac) in Obsidian and run "Brain graph: Rebuild brain graph from scratch".
- **For new brains, the first chat is shorter:** three questions, a short hello, and the computer extras offered once setup is done.
- **Updates will clash less.** Where your agents changed one of the kit's lines to fit you, your version now goes beside the kit's, in a place that's yours, so later updates leave it alone. This update sorts any it finds, once.
- **AnIdeaLab can send every brain a message,** for something everyone should know now, like a security fix. Your first chat after it's sent shows it word for word and asks whether to mark it read, so later chats don't repeat it.
- **The upload point is gone.** "My brain" in the right-click menu only worked when an agent remembered to read its list, so give your brain files by attaching them in the chat or saying where they are. Your agent takes the menu entry off your computer (or gives you one line to paste for it); anything still in its folder stays where it is.

Sometimes Claude's permission check, another AI, refuses that; your agent now tells you it was the check, not you, and asks for your yes in the chat.
- **Folders that get too full are split.** When a project's or area's front page gets too long because it lists too many notes, your agent moves a group that belongs together into a folder of its own, so the page stays quick to read. Your skills, tasks and history stay as they are.

When the tidy-up's done, your agent asks if you'd like to back up now: plug in your cold storage external hard drive, and it copies your brain and the files you choose onto it, never deleting anything on the drive and stopping if your files look like ransomware got to them. Say no and it's skipped. Nothing changes on your computer until you say yes.
- **Starting a new chat from where you left off is one click.** When your agent says a fresh chat can carry on, it gives you the line (a file's path, then "continue") in a box with a copy button, never a link that only opens the file. And it gives it only when a new chat is actually due (the chat's getting long, it has to stop, or you ask), not at every check-in.

Once a month the deep tidy-up lists it by size, and your agent brings you the biggest few folders: back it up, keep it but don't back it up, bin it, or delete it. - **Old chats from ChatGPT or Claude come in one chat at a time.** When you give your brain an export of your chats, your agent splits it into one file per chat first, so each one is read and filed, not skimmed as one huge file.
